DYRHALD

An underwater online game. Humans and fishmen share one ocean, push ever deeper, and slowly learn the abyss holds something older than either of them.

The story so far

A colony fell. You lived.

The surface died generations ago. People live below the waves now, in sealed domes and reef cities. Dyrhald was one of the few places where humans and fishmen lived side by side, until a blight rose from the deep and swallowed it.

You are one of its survivors, with no memory of the last night. Dead water is creeping upward, the reefs are bleaching, and every species blames another. The only way to the truth is down.

What you do

Dive, fight, carve, craft, go deeper

Each dive is a loop: head out, fight, gather what the sea gives you, come home, upgrade. The long goal is the next depth, where the water gets darker, colder and stranger.

Swim the whole ocean

One open sea you can swim and walk through, from sunlit sand to kelp forests and glowing caves. Diving is how you move, fight and escape.

Guns and blades

Harpoon guns, nail guns, scatter nets, gill shooters, spears, tridents and hammers. Swing, block, and land hits you can read.

Carve what you kill

No loot shower. Whatever you hunt, you carve by hand for scale, fang, meat and chitin, and better materials make better gear.

Gather and craft

Glowcoral, kelp, copper and iron ore. Every item has a quality and every material a rarity, from coral tools up through copper, bronze, iron and steel.

Your Den

A home base under the sea, with a forge, workbench, storage and a trophy wall. Sell from home, and set your hearth to wake there if you're knocked out.

A living sea

Named rare creatures in their lairs, fish migrations, predator frenzies, kelp blooms, and cultist raids on the town of Saltreach. Defend it, or don't.

Two species, one ocean

Tech or adaptation

Human divers survive on what they build: rebreathers, lamps, wetsuits and dive kit that grow with them. Their power lives in their gear.

Fishmen change their own bodies. Low-light eyes, deeper gills, pressure bladders: permanent adaptations with a cost.

Both species play together in the same world. The species changes how you see the story, not who you can group with.

Latest news

Release 12.2

4 November 2026

New

  • Meridian is rebuilt as a canyon city: dressed street walls and market stalls, shopkeepers behind their counters, a winch lift at Pem's, lit walkways and a rim you can walk.
  • Seven new Twilight armor sets: three role sets and four rare two-piece sets.
  • New cooking recipes.
  • Creatures fight with new moves and show a tell before they strike: the Shellback burrows and spins, ink clouds, spit, packs and schools, and thornback rays beat their wings.
  • Death markers show where you fell: a lamp for humans, a helm for fishman.
  • The diver's journal is a notebook with sketches, replacing the J journal and the Handbook tab.
  • Weapons, armor, rings and trinkets get new item cards: clear numbers, a side-by-side comparison with what you wear, and your set bonuses at a glance. The Knowledge tree, crafting and station screens use compact cards.
  • Sounds on every button and menu, new "learned" and "crafted" sounds, and a Menu sounds volume slider.
  • Footprints in sand and silt.
  • Glowing colonies of tube worms and anemones sit at the Twilight Shelf's glow spots, and glow lies on the ground instead of floating.
  • New layouts for Kelpwatch, Wrack's End and the Ledge Rats camp; a walk-in Brine Cup tavern; fishman watchtowers, salvage stalls and boat homes.
  • Log in by region: pick Central and Play takes you to the zone where you last played, with the start screen up until you are placed ("Connected to the Shallows · Central").

Changed

  • Zone hops show a loading screen, never the world in between, and a failed hop retries behind it.
  • Adapted divers swim off the Shelf's lip; divers who have not adapted are held at the lip.
  • The Twilight Shelf has a real twilight look, brighter and navy; the Rift is dark but never black and shelf caves are never pitch black.
  • The glowlamp is stronger (38 m) and its beam starts at your helm.
  • Pneumatic gun fittings open at Forge 1 (level 15) and Rail fittings at Forge 3 (level 28). Players who bought the old nodes get their points back.
  • Harpoon aim allows for the water's drop, with a hit marker and a hit sound: shots at creatures register far more often.
  • Side quests need the evolution and level 15.
  • Frame rate: far-away townsfolk and idle objects sleep, far lights switch off, far Meridian models are hidden, and menus draw faster.
  • Quest, NPC and game text is in American spelling.
  • The Rift is named once on the map, and Meridian no longer shows a square on it.
  • The Hunters' Run coral arch is gone.

Fixed

  • The garbled start-up sound and the crackle on the start screen, loads and server moves are gone; menu and game-made sounds no longer go silent after the sound system resets.
  • No more trenches, walls or cracks at terrain tile edges; 21 deep empty pits on the Shelf are filled and smoothed; rocks and 25 floating boulders rest on the ground; cave floors are set down.
  • The lip barrier pushes back again, and the server stops divers who have not adapted from crossing.
  • NPCs idle with their arms down and their feet on the ground.
  • An elite lurker no longer gets stuck in walls; the Chimaera's tail sways as it swims; the Shellback can't be swum through.
  • Small rocks no longer look like pale slabs; the Shelf cliffs no longer show stretched glow streaks; sun shafts no longer flicker.
  • Meridian: floor gaps at the Black Market door and by Warden Saal are fixed, the Den sits on the street, the border is repainted and edges blend into the street.
  • Buoys sit on rock piles with chains, camp props are solid, and Tidewatch and Station Nine stand on the Shelf only.
